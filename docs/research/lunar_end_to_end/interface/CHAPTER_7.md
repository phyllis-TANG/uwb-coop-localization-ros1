### Cross-segment comparison of peer-range models

Across the two Moon_1 segments, allowing a linear peer bias improved positioning under an injected 0.30 m range ramp relative to fitting a constant bias. On the 4.42 s segment, mean reductions in antenna-position RMSE were 6.07 and 5.11 mm for Cars 1 and 2. On the 4.34 s segment, the corresponding reductions were 5.63 and 4.92 mm, with improvements in 10/10 and 9/10 paired seeds. This contrast (M2 minus M3) measures the value of modeling a trend when both estimators already use the peer range. Against M0, which uses the same anchor and motion observations without a peer range, M3 reduced mean RMSE on the second segment by only 0.57 and 0.30 mm. This second contrast measures the net positioning benefit of adding the peer range with the trend model. Car 2 improved in only 4/10 seeds in this comparison. The 4.62 mm difference between its two mean gains reflects the additional error of fitting a constant bias to a ramp. These are descriptive paired results from two geometries of one synthetic sequence.

When the injected peer bias was zero, the mean absolute M3 slope under normal motion was 3.07 mm/s on the first segment and 4.88 mm/s on the second. Injecting one anomalous motion increment increased the signed slope relative to the matched normal observation set by an average of 0.90 and 0.97 mm/s, respectively. The fitted peer trend therefore also responded to motion-observation error.
